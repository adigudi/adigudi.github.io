The join everything depends on

Shopify gives you the return, the SKU and the cost. It does not know which production run the unit came from, so batch has to be resolved from the PO system by order date against receipt windows. This is the most fragile link in the system and the first thing I would check against real data, because wrong batch attribution means wrong vendor claims.

Where it deliberately stops

No photo, no claim
Gradeable from text, but never auto-billed to a vendor.
Unknown batch
Counts in quality totals, held out of the ledger.
New reason codes
Alerts instead of silently defaulting to a bucket.
Claim reconciliation
Claimed against received. A widening gap means a vendor is ignoring you.
Duplicate RMAs
Deduped so one unit is never claimed twice.
Low confidence
Anything ambiguous goes to a person, not to a number.

Working means it runs, on invented data. Designed means the output is specified and you can see exactly what it produces, but nothing is wired to send or write yet. Everything downstream of the grade is a day of plumbing each, and all of it is worthless if the grading is wrong, which is why the grading is the part I built first.

Why this is useful

Returns get treated as a logistics cost. They are also the only place where a customer tells you, with the product in hand, exactly what your factory got wrong. That signal is worth more than the credits.

Photos, sheets and vendor email are four manual steps One loop, batched weekly
Margin, not revenue
Recovered production credits lower COGS. It moves the cost of a bad unit off your balance sheet and back to the factory floor, where it belongs.
Vendor leverage
Next season's negotiation stops being "we feel like your quality slipped" and becomes a defect rate per factory and per batch, with photos behind every unit.
Consistent grading
Warehouse staff sort clothes well. They are not QA engineers. The same rubric runs on every return, so the signal stops depending on who opened the box.
Repeat purchase
Sizing returns are normal. A seam that splits on the first wash is not, and that customer does not come back. Killing a defect before the next run protects your best sellers.
